On March 8 there was the Columbia Young Scientist Expo, organized by the University of Missouri, and on March 13 the engineering department hosted an event for middle schools and high schools for Engineers week.
Besides the setups Dr. Young developed earlier about conductive polymers and the 'writing with light' and vacuum setups we developed last year, we also wanted to test some new outreach ideas.

- Using chains of rubber bands to show how sequence control can determine the properties of polymers
- Using UV-curable nailpolish to demonstrate polymerization in real time
- World's smallest football stadium: Andrew Reinhardt. printed [Faurot Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faurot_Field) with the [MU nanoscribe printer](https://msei.missouri.edu/facilities/nanoscribe-quantum-x-shape-high-resolution-3d-printer/) - and visitors could see a slightly upscaled version it with their own eyes using a stereomicroscope. He also printed downtown St. Louis!

While I helped brainstorm, guide along the way, and organize, credit really goes to the graduate students who realized the ideas from idea to development. In particular (for the setups mentioned) Andrew Reinhardt and Erick Guttierez Monje.
