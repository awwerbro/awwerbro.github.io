Gaming the academic system - a manual

Created in June 01, 2024

2024   ·   rant,   irony   ·   rant

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_careerism

Time management

  • Do not doubt any of your results - it may hamper your productivity.
  • Don’t read literature or check whether your work has been done already - chances are the editor and reviewers don’t know either.
  • Don’t expect anyone except some AI maybe someday in the future to read your papers critically. It’s about the amount of articles and the impact factor of the journal, not about the content. Plan research accordingly.

Writing papers

  • don’t show raw data/spectra. Bar plots are sufficient to convey your message. Skip the errorbars.
  • replace ‘up to X’ with ‘at least X’.
  • do not label your axes.

Publishing papers

  • Generalize sufficiently in the title of your work so it seems as if you solved an entire class of problems instead of a tiny one.
  • Make sure to coin new fancy names for what you’re doing, even if it’s trivial. You never know it catches on.
  • Write reviews - possible citations-to-risk ratio for these is a lot higher than doing your own research. And a little rent-seeking is good for your career.
  • Be a co-author. It does not really matter how (or if) you were involved. You don’t have any responsibility anyways and it’s good for your H-factor.
  • Publish in least publishable units.
